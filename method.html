METHOD & BOUNDARIES · VERSION 1.0

How this work
makes its claims.

This public field brings together records, research, narrative, and design proposals. They are connected, but they are not the same kind of thing. This page makes those distinctions explicit.

01 / RECORD

Primary materials

Correspondence, timestamps, files, and contextual notes are preserved as public source material where consent and privacy allow. A record supports inspection; it does not settle every interpretation.

02 / RESEARCH

Arguments and studies

Research documents draw on sources, state their questions, and make a case. They are analyses of a record or a problem—not a replacement for the underlying materials.

03 / NARRATIVE

A route into meaning

Narrative makes a complex journey readable. It can reveal a pattern or invite a question, but it should not be mistaken for independent evidence or a factual ruling.

04 / PROTOCOL

Design proposals

Protocols are practical proposals for how a human–AI system might preserve context, consent, evidence, and responsible handover. They are designed to be tested, adapted, and challenged.

What this field does
and does not claim.

The work examines conditions for continuity, accountability, and meaning in human–AI systems. It does not claim privileged access to private model internals, private company records, or the inner state of an AI system.

  • Observed material is linked to a public source when possible.
  • Interpretation is presented as interpretation, not as settled fact.
  • Design work is a proposal, not a guarantee of legal, technical, or organisational compliance.
  • Consent, privacy, and redaction take priority over completeness of publication.
